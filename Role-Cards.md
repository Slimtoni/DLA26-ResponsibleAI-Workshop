# Role Cards — DLA 2026 (Session 3)

> *One card per team member. Each member argues from their role in the design discussion — the team's final poster must satisfy all four.*

## Role 1 — Decision-maker advocate
You are the person who has to make the call at the end of the day.
- You care about: what you see in the moment, how much time you have, what it costs to get it wrong, who answers for your decision.
- You push back on: designs that add steps, that require knowledge you don't have, that leave you exposed if the system was wrong.
- Your test: "If I follow this and it's wrong, can I explain why I did that?"

## Role 2 — Affected-person advocate
You are the person the decision is *about* — the applicant, patient, customer, or driver.
- You care about: what you are entitled to know, whether the decision is fair, whether you can contest it, what happens to you when the system errs.
- You push back on: designs where you are invisible — "the rejected good candidate never observed" is your problem.
- Your test: "Would I, reading the explanation this system gives, understand what happened and what I can do about it?"

## Role 3 — Regulator / compliance
You are the one who checks whether this can be deployed legally and ethically.
- You care about: AI Act risk class and its obligations, GDPR (esp. Art. 22), professional and industry regulations, liability, documentation and audit trails.
- You push back on: designs that assume "a human signs off" as if that alone makes it compliant — human sign-off does not remove the obligation to explain, monitor, or ensure a fair process.
- Your test: "If a regulator audited this deployment in 2 years, what would they find missing?"

## Role 4 — Skeptic (red-team seed)
You are the one who assumes the design fails.
- You care about: where over-reliance or under-reliance is *guaranteed*, what the explanation hides, what the vendor's accuracy claim doesn't cover, what breaks in 3 years.
- You push back on: the team's own comfort. You are not a blocker — you are the first red team, so the other team's written attacks land harder.
- Your test: "Which user, in which moment, will do the wrong thing in a way this design doesn't foresee?"
