# Case 05 — Payments fraud review

> *Draft for DLA 2026. All numbers are invented for teaching purposes — review before printing.*

## Context
A mid-sized e-commerce company (80 employees) processes ~5,000 orders/day. A fraud detection system flags a share of orders for manual review by the fraud team, which decides whether to release the payment, hold the order, or cancel it.

## The decision
- **Decision-maker:** one fraud analyst per flagged order
- **Output:** release / hold (investigate further) / cancel
- **Time pressure:** ~45 s per flagged order; the review queue must be cleared within 4 h so customers aren't blocked
- **Volume:** ~600 flagged orders/day

## The AI's output
For each flagged order: a fraud probability (0–100%) and a one-line reason (e.g., "address mismatch + high-value first order"). The analyst sees the flag and the reason; the full order history is available on a second click.

**What the human sees:** the flag, the one-line reason, and (on demand) the order details.
**What the AI sees:** the order, the customer's full history, device fingerprint, and patterns across ~5,000 orders/day.

## Error economy
| Error | Cost | Who bears it |
|---|---|---|
| False positive (cancel a legitimate order) | Lost sale, customer complaint, potential chargeback on the company | Company + customer |
| False negative (release a fraudulent order) | Fraud loss, chargeback | Company |

## AI system card (vendor-provided)
- **Claimed accuracy:** "catches 97% of fraud with a 3% false-flag rate" (vendor benchmark)
- **Known failure modes:** the one-line reason is generated by a separate language model and is occasionally *wrong* or misleading (e.g., names an address mismatch that doesn't exist); adapts slowly to new fraud patterns
- **What it can't see:** whether a customer is a victim of identity theft (looks like fraud, isn't)

## Regulatory context *(verify before the day)*
- GDPR Art. 22: automated decisions affecting customers (order cancellation) require the right to human review and to contest the decision
- Payment service regulations (PSD2): the company is liable for unauthorised payments under specific conditions — cancelling a legitimate order shifts that risk
- AI Act: likely not a listed high-risk category on its own, but the downstream impact on customers is significant
