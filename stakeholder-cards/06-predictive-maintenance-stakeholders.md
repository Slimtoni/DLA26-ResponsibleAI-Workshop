# Stakeholder cards — Predictive maintenance

### Card A: Fleet manager (decision-maker)
- **Goal:** keep 40 trucks running, minimise downtime and service cost
- **Fear:** a false alarm costs a delivery day; a missed failure costs a breakdown on the road
- **Wants to know:** why the model keeps flagging more trucks (the false-alarm rate is rising); what it can't see
- **Entitled to:** a system whose performance they can monitor; the right to stop using it if it degrades

### Card B: Maintenance technician (decision-maker, second)
- **Goal:** do the right repairs without being flooded with unnecessary ones
- **Fear:** their judgement being overruled by a model that has never *seen* a breakdown; being blamed for following the model when it was wrong
- **Wants to know:** the sensor data behind each alert; to be able to say "this truck is fine" and have it count
- **Entitled to:** professional autonomy; their overrides logged *as expert input*, not as errors

### Card C: Logistics customers (affected person)
- **Goal:** on-time delivery
- **Fear:** a truck breaking down on the road and their delivery arriving late
- **Wants to know:** (usually nothing directly) — but in a major delay, they want to know why
- **Entitled to:** the service level agreed with the company
